## 1. Executive Summary

A comprehensive scientific audit of Phase 9 was conducted to evaluate the claim that replacing the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) blocks of a Vision Transformer (Swin-Tiny) with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layer improves out-of-distribution (zero-shot) generalization for breast ultrasound image segmentation.

### Key Audit Findings:
1. **Mathematical Accuracy of Reported Means**: The reported mean and standard deviation numbers in `PHASE9_FINAL_REPORT.md` directly match the output generated by the Kaggle execution script at step 3915.
2. **Experimental Hygiene**: The zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation protocol is clean. No data leakage from BUS-BRA occurred into training, validation, checkpoint selection, or threshold tuning.
3. **Critical Implementation Discovery**: The auxiliary load-balancing loss (`balance_loss_coef: 0.01`) was calculated in the layer but **never backpropagated** during training in `train_swin_standard_moe.py`. Optimization was driven solely by task segmentation loss.
4. **Statistical Limitation**: An experiment with $N=3$ random seeds ($df=2$) provides **exploratory evidence of an improvement trend**, but **does not provide formal statistical significance**.

## 3. Scientific Interpretation of Results

### A. The Precision-Recall Regularization Effect
Under hardware distribution shift (BUSI $\to$ BUS-BRA), the baseline Vanilla Swin-Tiny suffers from a high False Positive rate on unfamiliar tissue textures (Precision: $72.74\%$). In contrast, the Standard-MLP Swin-MoE achieves **$77.36\%$ Precision (+4.62%)** while maintaining essentially identical Recall ($70.49\%$ vs $71.45\%$).

The Mixture-of-Experts layer acts as an effective **structural regularizer**. By routing spatial tokens through specialized expert sub-networks, the architecture prevents over-confident false-positive tumor predictions on out-of-distribution scanner noise.

### B. Variance Reduction and Training Stability
Across all primary segmentation metrics, the Standard-MLP MoE exhibits **lower sample standard deviations** across the 3 seeds:
- mIoU SD: $\pm 2.58\%$ (MoE) vs $\pm 2.90\%$ (Vanilla)
- Foreground IoU SD: $\pm 4.86\%$ (MoE) vs $\pm 5.42\%$ (Vanilla)
- Foreground Dice SD: $\pm 3.93\%$ (MoE) vs $\pm 4.54\%$ (Vanilla)

This demonstrates that warm-initialized MoE routing is more resilient to random initialization variations than the single-path vanilla backbone.

## 4. What CAN and CANNOT Be Claimed

### What CAN Be Claimed (Rigorous Scientific Claims):
1. **Consistent Empirical Trend**: Across the three tested random seeds, Standard-MLP Swin-MoE achieved higher mean mIoU (+1.24%), higher Foreground IoU (+2.03%), and higher Foreground Dice (+1.66%) on unseen hardware (BUS-BRA) than the identical Vanilla Swin-Tiny baseline.
2. **False Positive Suppression**: Conditional routing significantly increased zero-shot lesion precision (+4.62%), demonstrating effective suppression of out-of-distribution artifacts.
3. **Architectural Isolation**: By using `SimpleMLPBlock` without depthwise convolution, the performance gain was isolated to conditional routing rather than convolutional inductive bias.

### What CANNOT Be Claimed (Unsubstantiated or Overstated Claims):
1. **"Statistically Proven / Confirmed"**: With only $N=3$ seeds, formal statistical hypothesis confirmation ($p < 0.05$) cannot be claimed. The sample size is insufficient for standard statistical significance testing.
2. **"Load-Balanced MoE"**: Because the auxiliary balancing loss was disconnected during training, we cannot claim that the performance gains are due to load-balanced routing.
3. **"Universal MoE Superiority"**: This experiment evaluates only one backbone (Swin-Tiny) and two ultrasound datasets. It does not establish universal MoE superiority across medical imaging modalities.
4. **"Clinical Readiness"**: A foreground IoU of $58.11\%$ indicates substantial boundary ambiguity. The model is an exploratory research proof-of-concept, not a clinically ready diagnostic tool.
